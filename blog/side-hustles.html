The Problem With "Side Hustle" Advice

Search "side hustle ideas" and you get the same recycled list that's been circulating since 2019. Start a dropshipping store. Drive for Uber. Sell digital products on Etsy. Take surveys for pocket change. Launch a podcast nobody asked for.

None of this is bad advice, strictly speaking. All of it is generic advice. And generic advice is the enemy of action because it doesn't account for the only thing that actually matters: you. Your specific skills. Your specific schedule. Your specific financial reality. Your specific combination of experience that makes you different from every other person reading the same listicle.

I was between opportunities three months ago and drowning in exactly this kind of advice. Scrolling through the same Reddit threads. Bookmarking the same YouTube videos. Saving the same Medium articles for later. Always later.

Then I stopped looking for ideas and started answering questions.

The 60-Second Audit

Side Hustle Copilot doesn't start with opportunities. It starts with you. Not your resume—your reality. What are you genuinely good at? Not what's listed on your LinkedIn. What do people actually come to you for? What hours are realistically available—not "I'll make time," but when are you actually free? What's your financial target—not "more money," a number? And what have you done in the past that people paid you for, even informally?

Answering those questions took about 60 seconds. But the act of answering them was itself clarifying in a way I didn't expect. Forcing yourself to articulate your actual strengths, your actual constraints, your actual goals—that's work most people never do. And it's the work that makes everything after it possible.

Then the AI matched my specific combination—not my job title, my combination—to five income paths I genuinely hadn't considered.

The Five Matches

1. Industrial Automation Consulting (Remote Micro-Engagements)

Forty years of factory automation experience. Siemens PLCs. Rockwell Allen-Bradley. HMI design. SCADA systems. Process optimization. I'd always categorized this as "employment"—something that required a full-time role at a facility. The AI saw something different.

There's a growing market for micro-consulting: small and mid-size manufacturers who need 2–4 hours of expert guidance on specific automation problems but can't justify hiring a full-time consultant or paying a firm's minimum engagement fee. A two-hour Zoom call diagnosing a PLC issue. A four-hour architecture review for a new line. Expertise delivered in targeted bursts, priced accordingly.

I hadn't considered this because I was thinking in terms of traditional employment structures. The AI was thinking in terms of skill-to-need matching. Different frame. Better result.

Status: Active. Already generating income. This was the first match that converted.

2. AI Prompt Engineering for Enterprise Teams

Building 17+ custom GPT systems and 12+ Claude applications gave me a skill I didn't realize was commercially valuable as a standalone offering: enterprise-grade prompt architecture. Not "write me a better ChatGPT prompt." Systematic prompt engineering for production AI systems—the kind that needs to handle edge cases, maintain consistent output quality, and integrate with existing workflows.

Companies are spending significant budget on AI tools but getting inconsistent results because their prompts are ad hoc. They need someone who's built production systems to audit, restructure, and document their prompt architectures. That person exists at the intersection of technical depth and practical deployment experience.

Status: Exploring. Two discovery calls scheduled. The market demand is real—I'm calibrating the pricing.

3. Technical Writing for AI/Automation Documentation

My specific combination of deep technical knowledge and clear writing style maps to a severely underserved market. Most technical writers don't understand AI architecturally. Most AI engineers can't write documentation that humans actually want to read. The intersection is valuable precisely because it's sparse.

This wasn't on my radar because I think of writing as something I do to communicate, not as a service. The AI identified it as a monetizable skill because the demand signal is clear: companies are shipping AI products faster than they can document them, and the documentation gap is creating support costs, churn, and adoption failures.

Status: Researching rate benchmarks. The hourly rates for specialized AI technical writing are substantially higher than general tech writing.

4. Course Creation: "From GPT to SaaS"

The dub-L-tap F1 framework—the 37-page methodology I developed for converting custom GPTs into standalone monetized web applications—is a documented, proven process that other builders want to learn. Not theory. Not "here's what I think might work." A specific, tactical playbook with nine production deployments as evidence.

The AI flagged this because the intersection of "has a proven methodology" and "has documented results" and "can teach clearly" is a course creation sweet spot. Most course creators have one of these three. Having all three is rare enough to command premium pricing.

Status: Outlining curriculum. The blog you're reading is part of the content strategy that feeds into this.

5. Hermetic/Philosophical Content Consulting

This was the wildcard. Mind Beyond Mind: IAMAUM—the book I wrote on Hermetic principles and metacognitive frameworks—positioned me at a very specific intersection that the AI identified as commercially viable: the bridge between ancient philosophical systems and modern technology.

There's a growing market of companies building what they call "conscious tech" or "mindful AI" products. They need advisors who can authentically connect philosophical frameworks to product design, content strategy, and brand positioning. Not New Age consultants. People who understand both the philosophy and the engineering. That Venn diagram has very few people standing in it.

Status: Waiting for the right opportunity. This is the highest-value, lowest-volume play—the kind that requires patient positioning rather than aggressive outreach.

What Made This Different

None of these five matches appeared on any "side hustle ideas" list I'd ever encountered. They couldn't have—they're specific to my exact combination of skills, experience, constraints, and goals. That's not a limitation. That's the entire point.

Generic advice produces generic action. Specific intelligence produces specific action. The AI didn't invent opportunities that don't exist. It identified opportunities in the gaps between skills I'd compartmentalized and never thought to combine. Industrial automation plus AI expertise. Technical knowledge plus clear writing. Hermetic philosophy plus production technology.

The combinations were always there. I just needed something to see the pattern I was too close to see myself.
